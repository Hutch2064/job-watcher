How Fieldnote works.

Fieldnote checks public employer job feeds and keeps a record of what it has observed. It is a discovery tool; the employer’s current posting and application are authoritative.

Matching your search

“Your role” uses words in the job title associated with investment research, portfolio construction, analytics, risk and related work. It does not make a complete judgment of the duties. Open a posting to assess its actual responsibilities.

Experience signals

Internship, summer and program titles are early-career signals, as are descriptions mentioning a minimum below three years. Senior titles and descriptions mentioning three or more years are set aside by default. A posting without a clear signal needs review; these labels are not eligibility decisions.

Cover letters

When available, the tracker reads the employer’s public application-question metadata or explicit wording in the description. A badge appears for a recognized required or optional cover letter. Many application forms are not publicly available, and an unlabelled posting may still require a letter or other written answers.

New, removed and stale

The first snapshot from an employer establishes a baseline and does not count its existing jobs as new. A posting observed later counts as new. A posting is marked removed when a subsequent successful snapshot no longer lists it. If a check fails or returns a suspiciously small result, the last known postings are retained and the source is flagged.

For example, if a board lists 100 jobs on Monday and cannot be checked Tuesday, those jobs remain in the tracker with a stale-source warning. They are not counted as Tuesday removals.

Market calculations

Charts use the saved history, not a complete census of all investment jobs. Observed time open is measured from the first observation to the latest removal and can understate actual posting lifetimes. Short histories make duration statistics especially tentative.
